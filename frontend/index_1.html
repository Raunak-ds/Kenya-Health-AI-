How it works

Three steps from the field to guidance. It works from a phone camera, with nothing to install.

1

Choose or capture

Take a photo of one leaf in daylight, or pick one from your gallery. You can also drag a file in on a computer.

2

Preview

Check that the leaf is sharp and fills the frame. Replace the photo if it isn't, and choose your crop and language.

3

Analyze

The image is checked, screened by the AI model, and returned with a confidence level, a severity estimate and guidance.

AI technology

Each photo goes through the same pipeline, built so that weak or unclear results are flagged rather than hidden.

  1. Photo quality check

    Rejects images that are too small, too dark, too blurry or that show too little plant tissue.

  2. Image classification

    A transfer-learning neural network trained on public leaf images identifies the likely condition.

  3. Calibrated confidence

    Temperature scaling adjusts the model's confidence, and a threshold chosen on validation data decides when to answer.

  4. Uncertainty handling

    Low confidence or scattered probabilities return "uncertain" instead of a forced guess.

  5. Severity and explanation

    An image-based severity estimate and a Grad-CAM heatmap show how much of the leaf looks affected and where the model focused.

  6. Guidance

    Management, prevention and monitoring advice comes from a curated knowledge base with source links.

Model candidates

Training compares three lightweight backbones and keeps the one with the best validation macro recall.

MobileNetV3-SmallResNet-18EfficientNet-B0

Built with PyTorch and served by FastAPI. Results shown in the app always come from the running model, never from fixed examples.

Trust and transparency

What this tool can and cannot do.

Decision support, not a diagnosis

A leaf photo cannot replace a laboratory test or an extension officer's visit, especially for severe or spreading disease.

Trained on public data

The training images come from PlantVillage, which is mostly taken against plain backgrounds. Results on field photos can be less reliable.

Severity is an estimate

It is a project-defined measure of discolored leaf area, not a validated agronomic score.

Language support is partial

You can choose English or Kiswahili. Parts of the interface and guidance are still English only.
